# Component A v2

The revised score averages expected points over a range of completed gains. It uses the pre-release depth, YAC, and completion models from the [validation experiment](README.md). It values the hypothetical next states with the public nflfastR EP booster and retains the snap clock and timeouts. Incompletions use the zero-gain state, with a turnover on downs when appropriate. Interceptions are grouped with other failed completions, so the score omits a separate interception cost.

## Gain distribution

Each outer fold holds out whole games from the weeks being scored. The three folds hold out Weeks 1–3, 4–5, and 6–8. Within the training games, three game-level folds produce out-of-sample residuals between realized completed gain and the sum of predicted catch depth and YAC. The script estimates 25 equally weighted residual quantiles, grouping by predicted depth and YAC. Groups with fewer than 60 observations use a broader pool. At each frame, it adds these residuals to the predicted gain and caps each scenario at the goal lines.

Completion probability weights the average point change across those scenarios. First-down and touchdown probabilities use the share of gain scenarios that reach the line to gain or end zone, multiplied by completion probability. The YAC number remains the fitted conditional mean. The displayed gain range includes variation in both catch depth and YAC.

Route rankings average the peak of a three-consecutive-frame rolling mean. If no three-frame window is available, the script uses the average over the observed frames. The player summaries average across routes, including routes with no positive window. The gain forecasts are tested on actual attempts at release. Early frames and untargeted routes are extrapolations. Rankings are intended to surface clips for review and reflect route usage, scheme, quarterback decisions, and coverage.

## Results

The revision scored **39,007 receiver-plays across 122 games**. On 2,282 held-out attempts in Weeks 6–8, the two approaches used the same fitted completion probabilities and nflfastR point model.

| Held-out metric | Single fitted gain | Gain distribution |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| First-down-or-touchdown Brier score | 0.2208 | **0.1870** |
| Touchdown Brier score | 0.0400 | **0.0350** |
| Signed pass-option value RMSE | 1.3053 | **1.2833** |
| Signed pass-option value MAE | 0.9593 | 0.9590 |

On Kupp’s Giants touchdown, the mean-YAC forecast was **6.2 yards** and he gained **13**. Two of the 25 completed-gain scenarios reached the goal line. With a 74.5% completion estimate, that yielded a **6.0% touchdown chance if targeted**, versus zero when the ball was spotted at the single fitted gain. The revised pre-release pass value was +0.054 points, versus +0.070 at the single gain. Averaging point outcomes can raise or lower the value even when it adds a plausible scoring branch.

Kupp’s mean route score was 0.508, and he ranked **84th among the 102 WRs with at least 100 routes**, compared with 79th under the original calculation. These are descriptive rankings of modeled options over different route assignments and game situations.

## Reproduce

Use the same nflverse inputs and Python dependencies as the [original validation](README.md#reproduce). Run from the workspace root, with `inputs` set to the directory containing those three downloads.

```bash
python3 "RB_Metric/validation/run_distribution.py" \
  --pbp "$inputs/pbp.parquet" --players "$inputs/players.csv" --ep-model "$inputs/ep_model.rda"
python3 "RB_Metric/dashboard.py" --results-dir "RB_Metric/results/v2" \
  --output "RB_Metric/receiver_dashboard_v2.html"
```

The scorer writes separate CSVs for receiver-plays, player summaries, and predictions at release on matched attempts. The held-out metrics compare the gain distribution to a single fitted gain using the same completion model and nflfastR point values. The evaluation uses Weeks 6–8 and excludes interceptions and lost fumbles from the binary pass-outcome benchmark.
